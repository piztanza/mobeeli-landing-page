R20 What else belongs on the front page

Two additions. Not five. And you have to earn the room.

You asked what cards, icons and stories could add substance without disclosure. I found six credible candidates and I am recommending two of them — because the strongest finding in the research is that stacking proof types is itself a tell.

“Don't stack four to compensate for having none.”

And the reason it reads as compensation: unverifiable claims and hard evidence look identical at a glance, so a reader discounts both at the same rate. Six vague sections do not add up to one credible one — they subtract from it.

There is also a hard constraint you did not ask about. Engineering measured the live page at 6.0 screens on mobile, with the catalog band alone taking 2.6 of them. Adding two bands without touching that puts you past seven. So the recommendation has three parts, and the third is not optional.

Three gaps, and the rule says fill one or two, not three. Founder evidence is the one to fill — partly because it is the cheapest, mostly because of what the research says about this specific market.

The Indonesia-specific finding

This market runs on people, not brands

Indonesian tech communications practice names a cultural value — guyub, gathering to build close relationships and trust — and describes Indonesia as a relationship-driven market where media, policymakers and the wider ecosystem place high value on face-to-face engagement and visible, named executives.

Your front page currently has one human being on it: a shop owner in Senen, quoted anonymously. Nobody from Mobeeli appears at all. /team exists, but a visitor has to leave the page to find out whether real people are behind this.

This is inference, and I will label it as such: the sources establish that Indonesia is relationship-driven and that named, visible leadership builds credibility there. They do not measure the effect of founder photos on a landing page. What they support is the direction, not a conversion claim.

The catalog band, on phones only. It is 2.61 screens on mobile against 1.52 on desktop — it does not just get taller, it gets proportionally 1.7× heavier on the device where patience is shortest. The picker, the scan and four part cards are all stacked vertically there.
