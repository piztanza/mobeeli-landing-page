R18 What belongs on the front page

Cut it. But not for the reason you gave.

You said the protection section doesn't need explaining on a page that should be a brief, deep statement of what you do. That's true, and it's the weaker half of the argument. The stronger half is competitive: trust is the one message in Indonesian auto aftermarket that is already taken.

Otoklix raised $10M to say “genuine parts, transparent pricing.” Bengkel Mania raised seed to say it too. If your front page leads on protection, you are the third company saying the same sentence.

Nobody in this market is claiming fitment. That ground is empty, it is the harder thing to build, and it is the thing you actually have.

So the recommendation isn't just “remove a band.” It's that the front page should stake the one claim no competitor can copy in a quarter, and let everything else — protection included — live where a serious reader goes next.

The market-size figures do not agree, and the spread is not small. Published estimates for the Indonesian automotive aftermarket run from USD 4.6bn to over USD 24bn depending on who is counting and what they count — parts alone, services alone, or both. Otoklix's own public figure is around USD 16bn. These are not reconcilable; they are different definitions wearing the same label.

That is a reason to put no market-size number on the front page. Any figure you pick is contestable by a reader holding a different report, and being caught with a soft number costs more than the number was worth. It also lines up with your own standing rule — quantity behind the gate.

The fitment statistics are not Indonesian. The 86%-of-returns and 20–30% return-rate figures come from US and global aftermarket sources. They describe a structural property of selling parts online — a Civic LX and a Civic Si take different brake pads everywhere — so I'd expect them to hold directionally in Jakarta. But I found no Indonesia-specific study, and if you cite them publicly you should say where they're from.
