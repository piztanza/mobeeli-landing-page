2 The profile is not the deck

You're right — and it's your own rule

R2 said it twice: keep the public profile broad, because the idea and the tech moat are easily copyable now; and the front page is 3–4 brief sections with fewer numbers — data lives on its own page. The page has drifted from both. I count twelve numeric claims on it today, four of them fabricated prices and three of them a labelled guess at the size of your database.

Show behaviour. Withhold quantity.

A statistic hands a competitor your scale and the shape of your data model — “specs / applications / models” is a diagram of how the fitment graph is built. A demonstration hands them the knowledge that it works, which they already assume, and which reading cannot copy. That's the line I'd draw, and it happens to be the line between a profile and a deck.

The architecture for this already exists — /deck is token-gated, /investors has a request form, the data room is behind a person. The front page is currently doing a job it already has a gated surface for, and doing it with numbers it can't stand behind.

04 Show it
saying no

The strongest thing in the round, and the one I'd argue hardest for. Band 2 only ever renders ✓ Verified Fit — four cards, four ticks, always. A badge that is never withheld proves nothing; decoration also always says yes.

Every mature fitment tool in the category ships the negative badge alongside the positive one. Mobeeli should show a part being rejected — not as a bad recommendation, but as the filter doing its job: 217 listings answer “brake pad”, four survive the Avanza's trim and engine.

✓ Standard Grade Pads front · 2NR-VE
✕ Ceramic Pad Set 1.3 E only

Red is used strictly as pain/error here, which is contract-legal. This is also the best answer to “make it look less like AI” on the whole list — generated pages are relentlessly affirmative. A product that tells you no is unmistakably a real one.

05 Aurora + ids
§4.6 · §4.7

Three full-screen shader contexts run permanently, at three different intensities, with no in-view gate — and the 183 KB three chunk sits in the hero's critical path. Collapse to one fixed aurora layer behind the whole page, revealed by the dark bands and covered by the light ones. Adjacent dark bands then read as one continuous space rather than two separately-decorated boxes, which is the actual fix for the catalog band and the archipelago sitting next to each other.

Intensity responding to scroll is the right instinct, but keep it on the existing rAF-writes-a-CSS-var idiom. animation-timeline: scroll() is not Baseline — Firefox still has it behind a flag and coverage is around 82–83% — so it can only ever be enhancement on top. Also drop preserveDrawingBuffer; nothing reads that canvas back.

Every band gets an id, and the DOM order changes so the problem band comes before the catalog. That fixes the scrollspy contradiction by correcting the page rather than the nav — and it's the better story anyway: state the problem, then answer it.
