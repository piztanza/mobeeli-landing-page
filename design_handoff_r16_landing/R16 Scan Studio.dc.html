Round 16 Scan studio · four live directions

Keeping the scan, but making it mean something

All four run live on a 5-second demo cycle: 1.8s of action, then 3.2s at rest, so you can see both the motion and the state it leaves behind. In the page it fires once per lookup and never idles. Every one is reduced-motion gated to its settled state.

The craft note that matters most

My first pass had the line decelerate across the whole traverse. That's wrong. A measuring instrument moves at constant velocity — deceleration reads as “coming to rest”, which is right at the end and dishonest in the middle. So the pass is now three phases with three easings: acquire 220ms (the line arrives and registers), traverse 1,100ms near-linear (steady, unhurried — this is the part that reads as measuring), settle 480ms (the values decelerate in, not the line). 1,800ms total, which is the timeout the component already uses — so this is a CSS change, not a logic change.

4a

The measured readout

· my recommendation

The line doesn't just pass over the car — it takes measurements, and leaves each one pinned to the point it measured.

⌀ 54.1 mm

Three dimensions land as the line crosses them, each on a leader line to the point it came from. The output is a small engineering drawing, not chips floating over a photo.

Why this one: it's the only version where the motion carries information. And the values aren't invented — fit3d_pcd_v (4 × 100) and fit3d_bore_v (⌀ 54.1 mm) are already stamped strings sitting unused in copy.ts. Offset would be the one new string.

It also quietly makes the moat argument the deck makes — fitment derived from real geometry, never typed in. A visitor sees bolt pattern and bore being read off a vehicle, which is a different claim from “we have a big database”, and a much harder one to fake.

4b

The focus pull

No laser at all. The car resolves from vague to exact — optical, not sci-fi.

A Toyota, probably
2019 Avanza 1.5 G · 2NR-VE

The image starts soft and desaturated with a vague label, and comes into focus as the exact vehicle resolves underneath. The whole idea of the product in one gesture: roughly your car → your car.

Most restrained of the four, and the least like anything else in the category — nobody in auto parts is doing optics. Cheap too: two properties, no extra markup.

Against it: a blurred hero image is a risk on a page where the founder brief asks for precision, and an animated filter: blur() on a large element is the most expensive thing in this file to composite. Would want measuring on a mid-range Android before it ships.

4c

The corpus collapses

· most conceptually unified

Don't scan the car. Scan the catalogue — and make the scan and the 217 → 4 the same gesture instead of two separate moments.

217 listings for “brake pad”
4 that fit a 2019 Avanza 1.5 G

Every candidate listing is a tick. The filter passes through them once; twenty go dark, four light up, and the count resolves underneath. The scan is the filter, so the band has one climax instead of two.

This is the strongest idea of the four and the biggest change — it means band 2 loses the car image entirely. Which may be a feature: a car photo is the most generic asset on the page, and the ticks are unmistakably data.

Against it: it's abstract. It needs the caption to land, and it gives up the one moment where a visitor sees their own vehicle. Also the largest build of the four.

Worth knowing: 4a and 4c compose. Measure the car, then collapse the corpus — 3.4s total across two beats. That's the version I'd build if you want the band to have a real sequence.

4d

The registration lock

Camera metering, not scanning. Brackets converge, snap, confirm.

✓ 2019 Avanza 1.5 G · 2NR-VE

Four corner brackets fly in and lock, then the confirmation snaps to centre. Fast, unfussy, familiar from every camera anyone has used — and it reads as identified rather than scanned.

Cheapest to build and the safest on performance — four borders and a transform. It also gives you a brand asset: those brackets are the same registration marks I'd use in the identity work, so the motion and the logo would share a vocabulary.

Against it: it's the most familiar of the four, so it's the least memorable. It confirms without ever showing you the work.

What I'd do

Build 4a. It's the only one where the motion delivers information rather than atmosphere, it reuses two already-stamped copy strings, and it makes the geometry argument visible — which is the part of the moat you can show without disclosing scale.

Then, if band 2 earns a second beat, add 4c after it. Measure the car, collapse the corpus. Two beats, 3.4s, one idea.

All four hold the same three rules: fires on a lookup and never idles · 1,800ms so no component logic changes · reduced motion goes straight to the settled state, which closes §5 #1 and the WCAG 2.2.2 failure regardless of which you pick.
